Michigan LLC Guide · Updated September 2026

How to start an LLC in Michigan without stopping at the paperwork.

Filing the LLC is one step. Building a business that can actually take a customer, get paid, stay organized, and keep up with its obligations takes a little more.

This guide walks through both: the basic Michigan LLC formation process and the practical systems you will want around it once the company exists.

The Short Version

What do you actually need to start a Michigan LLC?

At a high level, you will typically need to:

  1. Choose an available LLC name.
  2. Decide who will own and manage the company.
  3. Choose a Michigan resident agent and registered office.
  4. File Articles of Organization with Michigan LARA.
  5. Determine whether you need an EIN and tax registrations.
  6. Check licenses, permits, and local requirements.
  7. Set up business banking and financial records.
  8. Organize your domain, professional email, website, files, and customer process.
  9. Keep up with Michigan annual statements and other recurring obligations.

The exact steps depend on what your business does, but that is a much more realistic picture than “file an LLC and you're done.”

Step 1

Choose your LLC name.

Before filing, check Michigan business records to see whether your proposed name is available.

Also check your domain name, social handles, local competitors, and potentially conflicting trademarks. A name being available in the Michigan database does not automatically mean there are no other legal or branding issues.

If you already know you will use a shorter or different public-facing business name, find out whether an assumed-name filing or other registration applies.

Step 2

Decide who owns and manages the LLC.

Before you race through the filing screen, make sure the people involved understand who owns what and who has authority to make decisions.

If there are multiple members, questions about ownership, money, responsibilities, decision rights, exits, disputes, death, disability, and transfers are worth addressing early rather than waiting until everyone is angry.

This is one of the places where personalized legal and tax advice may be especially valuable.

Step 3 · Michigan Resident Agent

Choose a resident agent and registered office.

Michigan uses the term resident agent. If you have been searching for a Michigan “registered agent,” this is the role you are looking for.

Michigan LLC formation includes a resident agent and registered office.

The resident agent is the person or eligible organization appointed to receive official documents, notices, demands, and service of process for the LLC.

The registered office is the Michigan location associated with that role.

Your resident agent should be reliable because missing an important official document is not something you want to discover six weeks later.

Step 4

File the Articles of Organization.

This is the step that officially creates the Michigan LLC.

Michigan LARA currently directs domestic LLC applicants to its MiBusiness Registry Portal.

LARA identifies the domestic LLC formation document as CSCL/CD-700, Articles of Organization. You complete the form and pay the applicable filing fee through the state filing process.

Before submitting anything, review the business name, resident agent, registered office, organizer information, and other entries carefully.

Go to Michigan's official business filing portal →

Step 5

Decide whether you need an EIN.

An Employer Identification Number is a federal tax ID issued by the IRS.

Businesses may need an EIN for reasons such as hiring employees, operating as certain entity types, filing certain federal returns, opening a business bank account, applying for licenses, or establishing business credit.

The IRS issues EINs directly at no charge. Be cautious about paying a third-party website simply because it looks official.

2026 BOI Update

Do Michigan LLCs currently have to file a federal BOI report?

For U.S.-created companies, the answer is currently no.

FinCEN finalized changes to the federal Beneficial Ownership Information reporting rule in 2026. Under the current rule, companies created in the United States are exempt from BOI reporting requirements.

This is worth calling out because many older startup guides still say every new LLC needs to file a BOI report. That advice is outdated under the current rule.

Federal rules can change, so verify current guidance directly with FinCEN if you are reading this later.

Step 6

Determine which taxes and registrations apply.

Forming an LLC does not automatically complete every tax registration your business may need.

Depending on what you sell and whether you have employees, you may need to look at federal tax obligations, Michigan tax registrations, sales tax, withholding, unemployment, or other accounts.

Tax treatment can also differ depending on ownership and any elections the LLC makes.

Use official IRS and Michigan Treasury resources and involve a qualified tax professional when you need advice specific to your situation.

Step 7

Check licenses, permits, and local requirements.

Creating the LLC does not automatically give the business permission to perform every type of work.

Requirements can depend on your business activity, industry, profession, city, township, county, zoning situation, and whether you operate from home.

SBA guidance recommends checking federal, state, county, and city requirements because licensing and permitting obligations vary significantly by activity and location.

Step 8

Set up business banking and bookkeeping.

Keep business and personal money separated.

Ask the bank what documentation it requires, which may include your formation documents, EIN, identification, and ownership information.

Then establish a basic bookkeeping process for income, expenses, invoices, receipts, tax records, and recurring bills.

You do not need a giant financial system on Day 1. You do need a consistent one.

Don't Forget This

Michigan LLCs have an annual statement.

Forming the LLC is not the last filing you will ever think about.

Michigan LLCs generally file an annual statement beginning the year after formation.

The current due date is February 15. Michigan provides an exception when an LLC is formed or qualified after September 30: it generally does not have to file the statement the following February.

Michigan currently lists the LLC annual-statement fee as $25.

Put the date on your business calendar now. Do not assume you will remember it eleven months from now.

What You Don't Need to Do Immediately

An LLC does not need every business tool on the internet.

Build a giant website

Start with a site that clearly explains the business and lets someone take the next step.

Buy every software subscription

Start with tools that solve a current problem. Add more only when the business actually needs them.

Automate everything

Figure out the process first. Automate once you know what should repeat.

Look like a giant company

You need to look trustworthy and organized. You do not need to pretend you have 200 employees.

Michigan LLC FAQ

Common questions.

How do I start an LLC in Michigan?

Michigan LARA says a domestic LLC is formed by completing and submitting Articles of Organization to the Corporations Division with the applicable filing fee.

The state currently directs online filers through the MiBusiness Registry Portal.

Does Michigan call it a registered agent or resident agent?

Michigan uses the term resident agent. “Registered agent” is commonly used elsewhere and is also a common search term for the role.

Does forming a Michigan LLC give me a business license?

No. LLC formation creates the entity. Separate licenses, permits, professional approvals, tax registrations, zoning requirements, or other requirements may apply depending on the business.

Do I need an EIN for a Michigan LLC?

It depends on the business. The IRS says an EIN may be required or useful for employees, certain tax filings, business banking, licenses, and business credit.

Does a Michigan LLC have to file a BOI report in 2026?

Under FinCEN's current rule, U.S.-created companies are exempt from federal BOI reporting requirements.

Because this rule has changed substantially in recent years, check FinCEN directly for the latest information.

Does a Michigan LLC have to file something every year?

Yes. Michigan LLCs generally file an annual statement by February 15 each year after formation, subject to Michigan's exception for LLCs formed or qualified after September 30.
