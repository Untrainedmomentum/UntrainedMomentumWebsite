Business-service questions

Straight answers before we start.

I am not sure what service I need. Can I still contact you?

Absolutely. You do not need to diagnose the problem first. Tell us what is frustrating you, slowing the business down, or not getting done. We can help determine the next step.

Do you provide traditional 24/7 managed IT services?

No. Untrained Momentum provides practical technology support, projects, systems administration, documentation, coordination, and operational improvement. Businesses requiring a 24/7 help desk, security operations center, or continuous monitoring may need a specialized provider.

Can you work with our existing IT provider or vendors?

Yes. Vendor coordination and translating between business and technical stakeholders are core strengths. The goal is better ownership and outcomes—not replacing providers unnecessarily.

Do you work outside Michigan?

Yes. Most business services are available remotely throughout the United States. On-site availability depends on location and project scope.

Can you automate a process that is currently manual?

Often. We first understand the workflow, exceptions, ownership, and risks. Then we simplify it before automating anything. Learn more about workflow automation.

Can you help organize Microsoft 365, email, and shared files?

Yes. Support can include Microsoft 365 setup, permissions, account organization, shared files, collaboration, documentation, and practical administration. Learn more about Microsoft 365 support.

What is included in the website service?

Website plans start at $50 per month and can include hosting, routine maintenance, technical updates, and ongoing support. Website size, custom functionality, content creation, ecommerce, and recurring editing can affect final pricing. Learn more about website services.

Is monthly planning the same as unlimited IT support?

No. Planning services provide guidance, accountability, prioritization, and systems support. Hands-on implementation, troubleshooting, development, and third-party costs may require separate scope and pricing.
