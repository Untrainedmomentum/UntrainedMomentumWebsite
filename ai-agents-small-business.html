How It Works

Build the job before building the agent.

1. Identify the workload

We document what keeps repeating, who handles it now, what slows it down, and what a useful result would look like.

2. Choose the right approach

Some work needs an agent. Some needs a simple workflow. Some should stay fully human. We decide before adding technology.

3. Define access and guardrails

We decide what information the agent may use, what it may do, what it must never do, and when a person must step in.

4. Build and test

A basic agent is built around the approved use case and tested against normal work, incomplete information, and exceptions.

5. Document and train

Your team gets a clear explanation of what the agent does, how to use it, and what still requires human review.

6. Improve it

Results and exceptions are reviewed so the agent can be adjusted as the business learns what actually helps.

AI Agent FAQ

Questions before you give AI a job.

Will an AI agent replace an employee?

The goal is to reduce repetitive workload—not blindly remove people. Your employees still bring judgment, relationships, accountability, and knowledge of the business. An agent should support that work and give people more room for the parts that actually need them.

What is the difference between an AI agent and regular automation?

Regular automation follows fixed steps. An AI agent can interpret information and choose among approved actions within defined limits. Many good solutions use both, and some problems only need ordinary automation.

Can you build a custom agent for our process?

Yes. Custom agent scoping can include requirements gathering, workflow mapping, a basic build, testing, and a written scope when the work is more complex. Complexity beyond the basic build is scoped and quoted separately.

Does the agent get access to everything?

No. Access should be limited to what the agent actually needs. The design should identify approved information, restricted information, allowed actions, and the points where human review is required.

Can an AI agent make mistakes?

Yes. That is why testing, verification, guardrails, and human oversight are part of the service. AI output should not be treated as automatically correct.

Do we have to sign a long contract?

No. The work can begin as a defined assessment or project. Ongoing monitoring and improvement can be scoped separately when it makes sense.
